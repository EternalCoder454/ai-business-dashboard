# Eterneon

**A leadership team for a business that does not have one yet.**

Most AI tools give you one assistant that knows nothing about you. Eterneon gives
you eight heads of department who all know your business, argue from the same
facts, and remember what you decided last month.

---

## The idea

You write down what your business is, once. What you sell, who buys it, what you
charge, what you will not do, who you lose to.

Every head reads it. So when you ask Finance about pricing, it already knows your
margins. When you ask Legal about a contract, it already knows what you do. You
stop re-explaining your own company to a machine every morning.

---

## What you get

### A room full of people who disagree

Ask a question once and get eight answers, one from each area. Marketing wants
the launch sooner. Finance wants the price higher. Operations says you do not
have the staff. Your Chief of Staff reads across all of it and tells you what to
actually do first, and who to overrule.

That is the part no single chatbot can do. One opinion is easy. Knowing where
your own priorities conflict is the hard part of running something.

### Eight heads, shipped ready

Marketing, Social Media, Design, Finance, Legal, Operations, Engineering, and a
Chief of Staff who sits above them. Each one has a name, a role, and its own way
of working. Rename them, rewrite them, add your own, or give one to a person
rather than a department.

### It remembers

When you settle something, it gets written down. When a number changes, that gets
written down too. Both come back into every future conversation, so a decision
made in March is still holding in September and nobody has to remember it was
made.

### Briefings that arrive before you ask

Set a rhythm: every Monday, ask my Chief of Staff what the three things that
matter this week are. It runs overnight and the answer is waiting when you sign
in. Daily, weekly, or monthly, on whatever question you like.

Because the honest truth about tools like this is that you stop opening them.
Something being there when you arrive is different from remembering to ask.

### Work that goes somewhere

Briefs, pricing models, contract reviews, standard operating procedures. Save
them, keep them on a board, and export them as a **Word document** when you need
to send one to a person who is going to comment on it.

### A task board that fills itself

Ask a head what to do about something and it can put the task on the board while
you are still talking. Due dates, owners, and projects that keep a conversation,
its output, its files, and its tasks in one place.

### Your calendar, in the room

Connect Google Calendar and your Chief of Staff can see what is already booked.
"What should I focus on this week" is usually answered by "you have four hours of
meetings on Tuesday", and a head that cannot see your diary gives advice for a
week you do not have.

Read only. Only yours. Nobody else in your business sees it.

### Your own written record

An internal wiki that comes with 24 pages already written about how to get a
useful answer, what not to do, and who owns what. Edit them, delete them, add
your own. New staff read it instead of asking you.

### A library you build up

Eighteen ready-made ways of working: campaign briefs, decision records, project
retros, pricing models, contract reads, onboarding checklists. Give one to a
head and it uses that shape every time. Write your own for the things you do
your way.

### An inbox

Direct messages between your people, with who is online. It is not Slack and does
not want to be. It is there so a question about a piece of work happens next to
the work.

## Your team

Add colleagues by their work email. They sign in with Google, so there is no
password to set up and nothing to reset later.

Two levels, and that is all:

- **Member** uses the panel.
- **Administrator** also adds people, decides what they can do, and holds the
  keys the business runs on.

Somebody leaves, you remove them, and they are out on their next click. Not
whenever their session happens to expire.

---

## What it costs to run

You bring your own key from Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google, and you pay them
directly for what you use. There is no markup sitting in the middle, and you can
see the bill at the source.

You can also point different departments at different models. Put the expensive
one where the thinking is hard and a cheap fast one where it is not.

We work to keep that bill down rather than up: the parts of the prompt that never
change are cached, so a long conversation costs a fraction of what it looks like
it should.

## Your information

**Your business is sealed off from every other one.** That is enforced in the
code on every single read and write, and checked automatically so it cannot quietly
stop being true.

**The key you pay with is encrypted.** Not stored in plain text. Nobody can read one
back, including us, including you. Once it is set you can see whether there is
one and its last four characters, and nothing else.

**Sign in is Google.** We never see a password, because there is never one to
see.

**Your work does not train anything.** It goes to the model you chose, under your
own key, and comes back.

**Conduct review.** Internal messages are checked for harassment, threats, fraud,
and anyone who may be at risk. It does not look at your commercial information,
your client names, or your figures, and it does not judge tone. If you run this
for a team, tell them it exists.

---

## What it does not do

Worth saying plainly, so you can decide.

It is **advice, not a professional service.** Legal reads a contract and tells
you what to look at. It is not your solicitor. Finance looks at your numbers and
tells you what stands out. It is not your accountant. Both will tell you when
something needs a real one.

It **does not want your clients' confidential information**, and the heads are
told not to ask for it.

It **does not connect to your other tools yet.** No accounting, no CRM, no
scheduling. There is an API, so anything you can write can already read your task
list and write to it, and the catalogue of ready-made addons is being built.
